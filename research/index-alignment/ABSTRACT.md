# Boolean Index Alignment: Traceable Induction on Scales for Sparse Language-Model Systems — Abstract

**Manuel Coleman**
AgreeYa Solutions (Computacenter), Folsom, CA, USA · manuel.coleman@lovelabslca.com
November 5, 2026

Sparse language-model systems make discrete choices among experts, agents, retrieval locations, tools, memory paths, and provenance links. Coleman (2026a) called their mutual consistency **index alignment** and separated non-authoritative routers, state ledgers, and generative continuity experts. This paper turns that framework into a finite, evidence-conditioned certification construction. At each declared scale, four predicate families test routing (`A_route`), ledger commitments (`A_ledger`), generation and support (`A_gen`), and continuity (`A_cont`); their conjunction is `A(k)`. At a fixed specification and caller-supplied thresholds, authorization and compliance are boolean, while the monitor reports PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN, or NO_VERDICT and only PASS yields release bit 1. Missing evidence therefore blocks promotion without being misreported as an observed failure. A finite trace returns the earliest known failed scale, or on a causal DAG the frontier of concurrent minimal failures, each with a witness or counterexample.

Coleman (2026b) contributes an organizing order rather than an implication: sticky single-parent custody first, factoring of the non-sticky band, iteration-capped recursion with terminating or deadline-bounded subcalls, and residual accounting. The Kakeya theorems of Wang–Zahl and Guth–Wang–Zahl are background for that order; their asymptotic inequalities say nothing about language models, and booleanity here comes from explicit checkers and thresholds. We define typed index lineages, content-bound receipts, scale trees, claim graphs, correction frontiers, and governing acknowledgments, and we prove that sound local certificates lift across declared abstraction levels when evidence-preserving refinement (R), complete obligation coverage (C), noninterference (N), and correction-aware freshness (K) hold. Each global PASS then carries a finite certificate path to base evidence; local passes alone do not suffice, as an explicit shared-budget counterexample shows.

Non-sticky repair invalidates and rechecks the full affected dependency closure. `STRUCTURAL_READY` is separated from full release, which additionally requires cross-scale transfer, scope, independent authority and policy, and external support/fidelity. The decision object `INDEX-ALIGN-CERT` returns `{CERTIFIED, HELD_PARTIAL, REFUSED, AWAITING_HUMAN, NO_VERDICT}`, so authorized unaffected output survives a blocked full release. Verification of a supplied restricted certificate is linear in records and edges under bounded primitive costs; no complexity class is assigned to the underlying search problem. Fourteen worked traces and thirty-two mutation designs illustrate selected cases of MoE substitution, coverage-without-fidelity, stale tool authority, concurrent agent failures, correction storms, harness errors, continuity loss, and partial release, and an eleven-part evaluation protocol states how the construction is tested on real transcripts. Coverage is not fidelity, order is not authority, custody is not truth, and `Act = 0` does not entail `Stop`.

**Keywords:** index alignment; boolean certificates; traceability; induction on scales; sparse routing; mixture of experts; retrieval-augmented generation; provenance; correction propagation; runtime verification; failure localization.
